Technical documentation

How the county estimates are built.

The release estimates an average year during 2023–2025. It combines reported arrests, within-year reporting corrections, calendar-year imputation, reviewed agency relationships, and approved Florida and California sources.

01 · Scope

Average annual measures

Every headline arrest count is the arithmetic mean of three complete calendar-year values: 2023, 2024, and 2025. Rates divide that average annual count by the fixed 2025 population denominator. They are not pooled three-year rates and are not three-year totals.

2023–2025 average annual arrests = (2023 estimate + 2024 estimate + 2025 estimate) ÷ 3

The outcome is a custodial arrest event, not a count of charges, convictions, jail admissions, or unique people. Values are statistical estimates and should be read with each county’s coverage, stability, source, and imputation notes.

02 · Analytical frame

Current agencies and geography are held fixed

The analysis uses the reviewed 2025 roster of 14,573 independent general-purpose local police departments and sheriffs’ offices. That same roster, agency identity, service relationship, staffing value, jurisdiction population, covariates, and agency-to-county allocation are projected across all three years. The analysis does not attempt to reconstruct agencies opening, closing, merging, or becoming independent during 2023–2025.

FBI Originating Agency Identifiers are reporting identifiers, not always independent agencies. Current component ORIs and contracted jurisdictions are assigned to the reviewed service provider before monthly or annual reporting is assessed. Cross-county service areas are allocated using fixed 2025 population weights; the weights sum to one for each agency.

Population and race-specific denominators are the same 2025 analytical values used in the prior release, drawing on the 2020–2024 American Community Survey. No separate 2023 or 2024 population series is introduced.

Arrest year follows the arrest date recorded in NIBRS even when the record appears in a master file labeled for a different year. Those records are not assumed to duplicate records in another master file.

04 · Outcomes

Custodial and low-level arrests

NIBRS on-view and taken-into-custody arrests are retained; summonses and citations without custody are excluded. Group A and Group B records use the established deduplication rules. A person can appear more than once when arrested in separate events.

Low-level arrests use the same offense definition as the prior release: specified drug-possession, drug-equipment, gambling, prostitution, public-order, liquor, runaway, and trespass categories. Drug/Narcotic Violations count as low-level only when the available activity codes indicate possession or use and do not indicate trafficking or production.

05 · Reporter screening

Credibility is decided separately for each agency-year

An agency-year must contain at least three months with a positive NIBRS arrest count. Police agency-years with a positive officer count must also reach one corrected arrest per full-time sworn officer. Sheriffs remain exempt from the ratio screen because patrol, jail, court, and civil-process responsibilities differ substantially.

A police agency-year below the ratio before correction is not discarded immediately. It enters the screening within-year fit and is retained if the corrected annual total reaches the threshold. Agency-years that still fail after correction are excluded from the final within-year fit and from every later imputation training set. Qualifying SRS agency-years receive the same police-versus-sheriff officer screen after translation.

06 · Partial reporting

Persistent-underreporting correction within calendar years

NumPyro fits the monthly model separately in 2023, 2024, and 2025. This prevents information from crossing a December–January boundary while still pooling agencies within a calendar year.

A zero month or a positive count no greater than 10% of that agency-year’s median positive monthly count is treated as missing. Other months can also be adjusted upward when their posterior probability of belonging to a persistent underreporting state is at least 80%. Entry into the underreporting state is discouraged, while remaining in the state from one month to the next is comparatively likely; this favors coherent spells over randomly scattered corrections.

The likelihood uses agency population, staffing, type, seasonal and shared temporal patterns, overdispersed counts, and shrinkage. Flagged months are reconstructed from the model, with the reported count as a lower bound. Unflagged months are preserved exactly. No correction can reduce an agency’s reported arrests.

What the 10% rule does—and does not do

The 10% threshold automatically identifies fixed-rule missing months. It is not the upper limit on adjustment: another positive month may be adjusted when the persistent-state probability is high enough.

07 · Annual gaps

TabPFN fills missing aggregate calendar years

After within-year correction and final credibility screening, each qualifying source supplies a complete agency-year total. For an agency with one or two direct years, TabPFN predicts each missing full calendar year. The training rows are direct agency-years; the query rows are missing agency-years.

For qualifying 2023–2025 SRS agency-years that lack NIBRS, the same annual TabPFN bridge models translate SRS totals and composition to the within-year-corrected NIBRS scale. The selected specification combines peer agencies with clean same-agency history when available and falls back to a source-only peer model otherwise. Every year requires a complete 12-month SRS source record, bridge training is restricted to source/NIBRS overlaps below the 2× discrepancy threshold, and translated police counts must pass the post-translation officer screen.

Features include agency ID, state, agency type, primary county, calendar-year index, an agency-specific anchor derived from its direct years, and fixed 2025 population, location, race/ethnicity, poverty, and density covariates. Calendar-month features are not used because the target is an aggregate year. TabPFN runs on Apple MPS with one estimator.

Separate models predict the log total, low-level share, Black share within low-level and all-other arrests, and coherent non-Hispanic White, Hispanic, and other non-Hispanic composition within both offense strata. This produces total, low-level, Black, non-Hispanic White, Hispanic, low-level Black, and low-level non-Hispanic White counts that reconcile exactly.

08 · No direct year

Fully missing agencies are predicted on the three-year scale

An agency with no direct usable year does not receive three separate annual forecasts. Instead, models are trained on the three-year average annual outcomes of agencies with three direct years and predict the missing agency’s three-year average annual total and composition directly.

Features include fixed 2025 population, agency type, staffing, state, primary county, coordinates, percent Black, percent Hispanic, poverty, density, and a sheriff limited-policing indicator. The indicator is 1 only for sheriffs affirmatively designated limited policing through the reviewed research rules and 0 for every other agency, including unresolved sheriffs. Five-fold out-of-fold predictions determine aggregate calibration factors before the final fit. The same coherence constraints are applied to offense and race/ethnicity components.

09 · Population groups

Race and ethnicity composition

Black arrests include people reported as Black regardless of Hispanic ethnicity. Non-Hispanic White arrests require White race and non-Hispanic ethnicity. Missing race is distributed from known-race composition. The existing zero-Hispanic reporting audit and model remain in use for direct NIBRS agency-years.

When an annual total is temporally imputed, its composition is modeled rather than copied mechanically from one year. When the entire three-year average is imputed, total and composition are predicted together. Black and non-Hispanic White rates use fixed 2025 Black-alone and non-Hispanic White-alone population denominators.

10 · State alternatives

Florida and California fill gaps—and Florida can disqualify a NIBRS year

Selected Florida CJDT agency-year estimates replace national estimates when an agency-year would otherwise be temporally or fully imputed. They also replace a directly reported NIBRS agency-year when the corrected CJDT total is at least twice the corrected NIBRS total. This directional rule treats the much larger statewide count as evidence of major NIBRS underreporting; a discrepancy in the opposite direction does not by itself disqualify NIBRS. California OpenJustice county-year estimates replace county components that would otherwise include imputation. Each replaced annual component changes the three-year average by one third of the difference between the alternative and core value.

Florida agency detail is reconciled to the selected CJDT-based values and therefore sums to the county headline after county apportionment. California remains a county-level source, so no synthetic agency allocation is made; county pages identify that source and warn that agency rows may not sum to a California alternative-source headline.

Florida CJDT and NIBRS months first receive the same within-year correction. When clean same-agency NIBRS history is available, the Florida bridge predicts mean-centered monthly NIBRS counts from corrected CJDT months, peer agencies, and that history. Otherwise, an annual source-only TabPFN model translates the corrected CJDT record using clean peer overlaps. Annual composition is modeled separately and reconciled to the selected total. TabPFN is a pretrained tabular prediction model that adapts to the supplied training table without conventional task-specific neural-network training. Overlap rows with a twofold or larger discrepancy in either direction are excluded from bridge training. California annual values are translated for all three years with a peer-based source-only TabPFN bridge.

11 · Quality thresholds

County publication and stability

The general publication threshold is 80% usable-data officer coverage. An agency contributes its fixed officer count to the numerator when it has at least one credible reporting or selected Florida source year during 2023–2025; those values are allocated and summed within counties. California’s approved county source can publish a county directly.

Coverage = Σ(officers at agencies with at least one credible year) ÷ Σ(officers)

Publication and metric uncertainty are different. For each count numerator, a metric-specific NB2 dispersion is estimated from within-county variation across the three annual estimates. The variance of the three-year mean is the NB2 annual variance divided by three. The site reports an approximate 90% credible interval and a coefficient of variation for every metric.

A county metric is visually suppressed on the map when its coefficient of variation exceeds 30%. Its county profile still reports the estimate and interval with an unstable-estimate note. These process-noise intervals treat reporting corrections, source translation, temporal imputation, composition imputation, and alternative-source calibration as fixed; they do not incorporate those other sources of uncertainty.

12 · Published measures

Rates, percentiles, and rankings

Rates divide an unrounded average annual arrest estimate by the fixed 2025 relevant population and multiply by 10,000. Black–White differences subtract the non-Hispanic White rate from the Black rate.

For each metric, percentile ranks and reference quartiles are defined using published counties at or below the 30% NB2 coefficient-of-variation threshold. Higher-uncertainty estimates are located in that reference distribution without influencing it. Percentiles are capped at 1 and 99.

County pages expose the mean annual contribution of cross-county allocation, source translation, within-year correction, temporal or fully missing-agency imputation, and state alternative sources when those components apply.

13 · Interpretation

Limitations

  • Administrative data can contain coding errors, delayed submissions, incorrect identifiers, and inconsistent local practices.
  • Persistent-state reconstruction can mistake a genuinely low-enforcement spell for underreporting, particularly when local context is unavailable.
  • The one-arrest-per-officer screen can reject a genuinely low-arrest police agency. It is a data-quality rule, not a judgment about performance.
  • Agency existence, independence, service areas, staffing, population, and covariates are intentionally held at their current 2025 analytical values rather than reconstructed historically.
  • SRS, Florida detention records, and California county data do not exactly match individual-level NIBRS. Calibration reduces but cannot remove those scope differences.
  • Florida agency rows reflect selected CJDT replacements. California is available only at county level, so agency rows may not sum to a California alternative-source county headline.
  • County-specific intervals cover only estimated NB2 process noise. Coverage and stability flags are practical quality indicators, not complete uncertainty measures.
  • Arrest rates reflect enforcement, laws, policy, resources, reporting practices, and community conditions; they are not direct measures of crime prevalence or causal agency performance.

Interpret with context

Use source notes, coverage, stability cautions, and agency-level detail alongside every county estimate.
