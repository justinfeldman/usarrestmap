Technical documentation

How the county estimates are built.

The estimates combine reported arrests, reviewed agency relationships, statistical corrections, and alternative state data. This page explains the choices that turn those inputs into comparable county measures—and where uncertainty remains.

01 · Scope

Overview

This site presents estimates of arrests made during calendar year 2025 by general-purpose local police departments and sheriffs’ offices in the 50 states and the District of Columbia. Estimates are constructed first for each independently operating law-enforcement agency and then apportioned and aggregated to counties.

The objective is to produce comparable county estimates despite incomplete national reporting, differences between federal reporting systems, partial-year submissions, and local contracting arrangements. The values are statistical estimates rather than audited administrative totals. They should be interpreted with the coverage and stability indicators shown throughout the site.

Central distinction

An FBI reporting identifier is not always an independent police agency. The analysis first reconstructs independent service providers, then evaluates their reporting and estimates their arrests.

The roster excludes state police, federal agencies, campus and school police, transit police, airport and port police, tribal agencies, corrections agencies, and other special-purpose agencies. Agencies determined to be inactive, dissolved, or no longer independently operating are also excluded.

Sheriffs’ offices remain included even though their responsibilities vary. Some provide extensive patrol services; others devote more staff to courts, jails, civil process, or specialized functions. That heterogeneity is considered when staffing and arrests-per-officer values are evaluated.

03 · Agency identity

Independent agencies, ORIs, and contracting

An FBI Originating Agency Identifier, or ORI, is a reporting identifier—not necessarily an independently operating police agency. One agency may report through multiple ORIs, and an ORI bearing a municipality’s name may remain active after that municipality contracts with another department or sheriff.

The roster therefore uses the independent service provider rather than the raw ORI as its unit of analysis. It links 14,866 current reporting or service ORIs to 14,573 independent agencies. Of those agencies, 114 have more than one current ORI, accounting for 293 ORIs beyond their primary provider identifiers.

Relationships were identified from locally preserved research using government and agency records, staffing surveys, state and local contracting information, and, where necessary, local news reporting. Each decision and its rationale are retained in the research database.

For a contracted municipality or component reporting ORI:

  1. Its current ORI is attached to the actual service provider.
  2. Arrest records from all attached ORIs are summed before reporter status or annual estimates are determined.
  3. The former or component agency does not receive a separate imputed arrest total.
  4. Its service geography remains attached to the provider so arrests can be apportioned to the correct counties.
  5. Inactive ORIs are retained for provenance but excluded from current reporting views.

Preventing population double counting

If the provider’s Census geography already represents its complete service area, nested member populations are not added again. If the provider serves distinct noncounty jurisdictions not contained in its own geography, unique member geographies are summed. Identical Census geography sets are counted only once.

05 · Outcome definitions

What counts as an arrest?

The target measure is a custodial arrest event, not every enforcement contact reported in an arrest-related system. NIBRS distinguishes an on-view arrest without a warrant or prior incident report (O), a person taken into custody based on a warrant and/or prior incident report (T), and a summons or citation without custody (S).

The estimates retain O and T arrests and exclude S summonses and citations because agencies report them inconsistently. NIBRS does not permit T arrests to be separated reliably into warrant-only and prior-incident-report arrests.

One NIBRS row represents one counted custodial arrestee record. For Group A offenses, count-arrestee and single-incident records are retained while additional multiple-incident representations of the same arrestee are excluded. Group B records are deduplicated using ORI, arrest transaction number, arrest date, and arrestee sequence number.

A person may appear more than once if arrested in separate events. Arrest totals are therefore neither unique-person counts nor counts of charges, convictions, or jail admissions.

Low-level arrests

Each NIBRS custodial arrest is classified using its primary or most serious arrest offense. “Low-level” is an analytical category, not a judgment about the statutory grade or consequences of an individual case.

06 · Population groups

Race and ethnicity

  • Black arrests include arrestees reported as Black or African American, regardless of Hispanic ethnicity. The denominator is the ACS Black-alone population.
  • Non-Hispanic White arrests include arrestees reported as White and not Hispanic or Latino. The denominator is the ACS non-Hispanic White-alone population.
  • Black–White rate differences equal the Black rate minus the non-Hispanic White rate. They are differences per 100,000 residents, not ratios.

Race and ethnicity are not complete in every source. For credible NIBRS reporters, directly observed Black shares among arrestees with known race are used when available. Missing race is implicitly distributed according to estimated known-race composition. For agencies without usable NIBRS race detail, Black shares are predicted with the TabPFN procedure described below.

Missing ethnicity among arrestees reported as White may be divided between Hispanic and non-Hispanic White, but a person reported as Black or another non-White race is not reclassified as Hispanic merely because ethnicity is missing.

An agency is flagged for possible Hispanic underreporting when it reports no Hispanic arrests and either comparable nearby agencies have a median Hispanic share above 5% or the agency’s jurisdiction is more than 5% Hispanic. When applicable, an offense-specific model estimates the Hispanic share within the combined White/Hispanic pool. Unknown race is allocated separately between non-Hispanic White and other non-Hispanic categories using agency, state-offense, and national-offense fallbacks. Final margins are constrained to reconcile to the agency total.

07 · Staffing

Officer-count selection

The analysis focuses on full-time sworn officers. Part-time sworn counts are retained and used to investigate unusual or missing full-time values, but are not added to the primary officer denominator.

Staffing observations were compared across LEE 2020–2024, 2020 LEMAS, 2018 CSLLEA, and 2022 ASPEP. The ASPEP record retains whether a value was reported or imputed. When sources generally agreed, the most recent positive LEE count was preferred.

Strong disagreements were audited for crosswalk errors, obsolete ORIs, parent-agency staffing assigned to a former local agency, inclusion of jail staff, and implausible staffing relative to population or arrests. When disagreement remained, the source producing the more plausible combination of officers per resident and arrests per officer was selected or the case was marked ambiguous. Sheriff comparisons were treated more cautiously because sources differ in their treatment of jail, court, and civil-process staff.

Agencies without an available positive full-time count are audited separately and are not silently assigned zero officers.

08 · Data eligibility

Credible national reporters

Reporter status is determined only after all current ORIs belonging to the same independent agency have been consolidated. An agency initially qualifies as a credible NIBRS reporter if it has at least three months in 2025 with one or more qualifying custodial NIBRS arrests. A historical participation flag or directory listing is not sufficient.

A second plausibility rule is applied after within-year underreporting correction. A non-sheriff police agency is not considered a usable reporter if its corrected annual count is less than one arrest per selected full-time sworn officer. Its report is then treated as unusable for estimation and coverage. Sheriffs are exempt because their staffing and functions are too heterogeneous for the same threshold to be reliable.

An agency does not need twelve positive months. Missing or abnormally low months are addressed through the within-year correction. For agencies without credible NIBRS, ASR/SRS is usable when the agency has no positive NIBRS months and at least three positive ASR/SRS months. Agencies with neither source proceed to Florida alternative data, where available, or national missing-agency imputation.

09 · Partial-year reporting

Within-year underreporting correction

Some agencies submit credible counts for part of the year but report zero or unusually low counts in other months. Rather than annualizing the number of active months, the analysis uses a hierarchical monthly state-space model.

The model estimates an agency’s expected monthly trajectory from:

  • its typical arrest level;
  • population, agency type, location, staffing, and reporting source;
  • a shared national monthly path;
  • three shrinkable time-varying factors; and
  • a tightly regularized agency-specific monthly path.

Counts follow a negative-binomial model, which permits more month-to-month variation than a Poisson model. Estimation is implemented in NumPyro, a probabilistic-programming library for Python and JAX.

A month is treated as potentially missing when its count is zero or no more than 10% of that agency’s median positive monthly count.

Flagged months are excluded from the model likelihood and reconstructed from adjacent months, agency characteristics, and shared temporal patterns. Their reported count is retained as a lower bound. Every other reported month—including large upward spikes—is preserved exactly. A sparse surge component prevents legitimate unusually high months from being pulled downward automatically.

The result is an expected count for a reporting gap, not a claim to have recovered the exact number of arrests. Florida CJDT monthly event counts receive the same type of correction before source translation.

10 · Harmonization

Source translation and imputation

Tabular Prior-Data Fitted Network (TabPFN) is a pretrained neural network designed for supervised prediction with tabular data. In this project it is used to translate SRS and Florida data to NIBRS-equivalent measures, impute agencies with no usable arrest data, and estimate Black arrest shares when usable NIBRS race detail is unavailable.

ASR/SRS translation

ASR/SRS summaries do not measure arrests exactly like the filtered individual-level NIBRS records. TabPFN models are trained on credible NIBRS agencies for which both exact NIBRS custodial counts and SRS-format aggregates are available.

Separate models estimate the NIBRS-equivalent annual total and low-level share. Predictors include SRS total arrests, its observable low-level share, population, agency type, staffing, geography, poverty, density, and local demographic composition. The model first translates the reported SRS total to the reported-NIBRS scale. That result is then multiplied by the agency’s own within-year corrected-to-reported SRS ratio, keeping source-system differences separate from reporting outages.

Because SRS provides separate race and ethnicity margins rather than a joint table, additional models estimate the non-Hispanic White and Hispanic portions. Final components are constrained to sum to the translated total.

Agencies with no usable arrest source

For agencies without usable NIBRS, SRS, or qualifying Florida data, TabPFN models trained on credible NIBRS reporters estimate the full set of measures. Separate models predict total arrests on a logarithmic scale, low-level share on a log-odds scale, Black shares, and race/ethnicity composition within low-level and all-other arrests.

Predictors include population, police-versus-sheriff status, latitude and longitude, full-time officers, state, primary county, percent Black, percent Hispanic, poverty, and population density.

Estimated low-level arrests = calibrated total arrests × calibrated low-level share

Race and ethnicity are predicted as shares and multiplied by the corresponding offense total. Multiplicative raking makes the components reconcile exactly to the total, offense, and race/ethnicity margins. Calibration uses held-out agencies from counties that initially had complete coverage. Where possible, agencies accounting for approximately 20% of county officers were withheld, approximating the missingness faced at the 80% publication threshold.

A fully imputed agency contributes to a published county estimate, but does not count as a usable-data reporter in the coverage calculation.

Black arrest shares

Directly observed Black shares are retained for credible NIBRS agencies with known race data. For other national agencies—including SRS reporters and agencies with no usable report—a separately validated TabPFN share model uses agency characteristics to estimate total and low-level Black shares. Florida and California use their source-specific procedures described below.

Florida alternative data

Florida CJDT files are charge-level rather than event-level. Processing selects 2025 records, uses booking date only when arrest date is absent, removes exact duplicates and duplicate charge keys, maps arresting ORIs to independent agencies, and reconstructs events using agency, person identifier, and arrest date. Charge ID is a conservative fallback when person ID is missing.

To approximate custodial NIBRS arrests, the filtered event count excludes records consisting only of failure to appear, fugitive or out-of-county warrants, probation or parole violations, contempt, generic warrants, explicit “not an arrest” records, or routine traffic. DUI, hit-and-run, fleeing or eluding, and vehicular homicide or manslaughter are retained.

Corrected monthly CJDT features and agency characteristics enter TabPFN models that estimate NIBRS-equivalent totals, low-level shares, and racial and ethnic composition. Training was limited to 116 dual-source agencies whose corrected CJDT and NIBRS totals were not sharply inconsistent. Twenty agencies with a twofold-or-larger discrepancy in either direction were excluded from training. Their NIBRS estimate was retained when corrected NIBRS was higher; CJDT-based metrics were used when corrected CJDT was higher. Of 130 final CJDT candidates, 126 passed the minimum adjusted arrest-per-officer rule.

California alternative data

California OpenJustice provides county—not agency—arrest data. The latest statewide file used here covers 2024, creating a one-year source-to-target lag. Its categories do not reproduce the site’s low-level definition exactly.

Both TabPFN and a simpler calibration were evaluated for California. The simple calibration was selected for production; California’s final county estimates do not use TabPFN. The procedure scales the OpenJustice total by the aggregate target-to-source ratio and applies arrest-weighted log-odds offsets to its misdemeanor-plus-status, Black, and non-Hispanic White shares.

Total and low-level calibration used 37 counties with complete usable-data officer coverage; race-specific calibration used 23 counties with stable race estimates. Counties where OpenJustice and the national estimate differed by more than a factor of two were excluded from training. The method publishes 10 otherwise-suppressed California counties. Because the source is county aggregated, no synthetic agency allocation is created; the calibrated county estimate replaces the county total.

11 · Model checks

Validation

K-fold cross-validation divides observations into k groups, repeatedly trains on k−1 groups, and evaluates predictions for the omitted group. It measures performance on agencies or counties not seen during fitting.

  • Five-fold held-out-agency validation was used for SRS-to-NIBRS translation.
  • Ten-fold county-grouped validation was used for Florida so agencies from the same county did not appear in both training and testing.
  • Ten-fold held-out-county validation was used for California.
  • Structured agency and county holdouts, including simulated 80% officer coverage, were used for the national missing-agency models.

Validation examined total counts, low-level shares, racial and ethnic composition, rates, and Black–White differences. Performance was judged acceptable before the models were used in production. Cross-validation tests prediction for observed agencies under simulated missingness; it cannot establish that every genuinely nonreporting agency is estimated accurately.

12 · Geography

County allocation and aggregation

Agency arrests and officers are apportioned to counties using reviewed service geographies. A county or county subdivision maps directly to its county. A Census place crossing county lines is divided according to the 2020–2024 ACS population of each place-within-county component. Components containing less than 1% of the place’s population are dropped and the remaining weights are renormalized.

The same weights are applied to arrest and officer counts. Agency totals are thus preserved when an agency crosses county lines. Bronx, Kings, New York, Queens, and Richmond counties are combined into a single New York City analytical unit.

Within each county, the final estimate directly sums:

  • adjusted NIBRS agency estimates;
  • NIBRS-equivalent SRS estimates;
  • qualifying Florida estimates; and
  • model-based estimates for agencies with no usable data.

No inverse-coverage multiplier or officer-based scaling is applied after these values are summed. California alternative counties are the exception because their final values come from a direct county-level source.

13 · Quality thresholds

County publication and metric stability

Officer-based coverage

The general publication threshold is 80% usable-data officer coverage.

Coverage = apportioned full-time officers at usable-data agencies ÷ all known apportioned full-time officers serving the county

Usable sources are NIBRS, SRS, and qualifying Florida data. Coverage is not the percentage of agencies, residents, or arrests observed. Generic model-only imputations do not enter the numerator. A county meeting the threshold may still include imputed agencies, whose estimates are included in its total.

Counties below 80% are suppressed even though provisional estimates may exist internally. The California county-source procedure can publish a county directly when national-data officer coverage remains below 80%.

The current release publishes 2,523 county units and suppresses 607. Of the published units, 888 include at least one nationally imputed agency and 10 use the California calibration.

Metric-level instability

Suppression and instability are different. Within a published county, a metric is marked unstable when the county has fewer than 250 arrests in the underlying reported source data, before within-year correction or generic imputation. The source count is observed NIBRS arrests, reported SRS arrests, filtered Florida substantive events, or raw California OpenJustice arrests, as applicable.

Black arrest rates, Black low-level rates, and both Black–White differences receive an additional instability flag when the county has fewer than 100 estimated Black arrests. This criterion is applied only to Black-related metrics. There is no minimum estimated-total-arrest rule.

An unstable value may still be displayed with a caution and included in rankings when the user requests it. A rate is unavailable when its population denominator is zero or missing.

14 · Published measures

Rates, percentiles, and rankings

Every rate is reported per 100,000 relevant residents:

  • overall rate = total estimated arrests ÷ total population × 100,000;
  • low-level rate = estimated low-level arrests ÷ total population × 100,000;
  • Black rate = estimated Black arrests ÷ Black-alone population × 100,000;
  • non-Hispanic White rate = estimated non-Hispanic White arrests ÷ non-Hispanic White-alone population × 100,000; and
  • Black–White difference = Black rate − non-Hispanic White rate.

Estimates may be fractional internally because of modeling and cross-county apportionment. Displayed arrest counts are rounded to the nearest whole number, but rates are calculated from unrounded values.

For each metric, the national percentile distribution is defined using only published counties whose value is stable for that metric. Unstable estimates are then located within that stable distribution without influencing it. Percentiles are capped at 1 and 99; the site never reports 0 or 100.

Map colors use these percentiles. The ranking score is a weighted average of the selected metric percentiles. Equal weight is assigned by default to overall and low-level rates, Black overall and low-level rates, and the total and low-level Black–White rate differences. Percentile scoring prevents a few extreme raw rates from dominating the map or composite ranking.

Audit information shown for counties

When applicable, a county page reports the share of estimated arrests affected by:

  • cross-county apportionment;
  • SRS-to-NIBRS translation;
  • Florida CJDT translation;
  • within-year underreporting correction;
  • Hispanic-ethnicity imputation;
  • imputation for agencies with no usable arrest data; and
  • California OpenJustice calibration.

A caution that does not apply to a county is omitted from its page.

15 · Interpretation

Limitations

  • Administrative data can contain coding errors, delayed submissions, incorrect ORI assignments, and inconsistent local practices.
  • Treating zero or extremely low months as reporting gaps can overestimate arrests for an agency that genuinely made few or no arrests during those months.
  • The one-arrest-per-officer screen can classify a genuinely low-arrest police department as unusable. It is a data-quality rule, not a judgment about agency performance.
  • Sheriff responsibilities and staffing definitions differ substantially across counties and data sources.
  • SRS, Florida detention records, and California county data do not have exactly the same scope as individual-level NIBRS. Statistical translation reduces but cannot remove those differences.
  • California estimates use a 2024 source to approximate 2025 measures.
  • ACS denominators are 2020–2024 period estimates rather than exact 2025 populations.
  • County-specific confidence intervals are not currently reported. Coverage and instability flags are practical quality indicators, not complete uncertainty measures.
  • Arrest rates reflect enforcement activity as well as behavior, laws, policy, resources, reporting practices, and community conditions. They are not direct measures of crime prevalence or causal agency performance.

Interpret with context

Use the source notes, coverage measure, stability cautions, and agency-level detail alongside every county estimate.
